What matters

She relaunched Meta on 20 Aug &mdash; 18 ads, all in one day

The 31 July read that she buys zero Meta traffic was true when taken and is now wrong. On 20 Aug she pushed 18 live ads / 7 distinct creatives from page 1021090297763539, every one pointing at a slug we had never seen: /signup. They were 10 hours old at capture and 5 of 13 cards were flagged under 100 impressions — a relaunch in testing, not a scaled account. The media is 84 days old, so she is re-running May creative rather than shooting new. gethookd still returned 0 for her a full day after the relaunch — the third-party index lags Meta's own library, so a gethookd zero is unproven, not absence.

6 of the 7 live creatives sell the wrong class

Six of the seven running videos say “this Sunday at 3pm Eastern”. All 18 ads land on /signup, whose own title is “Countdown — Next Wed 8pm ET”. Only one creative says Wednesday. She is paying to promise a day her landing page contradicts on arrival.

Two pixels, two operators

/signup, /almostdone and /replay carry pixel 2548125322312559 plus a Cortana hub, and a St. Petersburg, FL footer. /start and /application carry 903499129224608, no Cortana, and a Los Angeles, CA footer. A domain-wide Ad Library query returns no ads at all pointing at the second funnel, so whatever feeds it is not Meta.

The $13 replay is now free to anyone with the URL

She sells replay access for $13 inside the confirmation ladder, then serves the entire 1h 42m class unauthenticated on /replay, with a 1-hour “Miss Affiliate 1-1 Call” booking calendar directly beneath it.

The application carries a hard $2,000 budget floor

Typeform JAYokipv, 10 questions. Q6 writes the DQ into the answer itself: “I have less than $2,000 (this is not the right fit for me at this time)”, against bands of $2,000–$5,000 and +$5,000. Two separate questions make her commit to showing up, on a computer, for 45–60 minutes.

She runs follow-up on SMS, not email

Signed up twice on two different addresses and no confirmation email ever arrived. Her confirmation page says instead: “We just texted from +1 757-580-9956, reply YES to confirm.” Her whole follow-up runs by text. This is the closest competitor comparison we have to AI-LNS.

Her join link is hidden in the calendar button

The confirmation page never prints the join URL. It is buried inside the “Add to Google Calendar” link as an encoded parameter: event.webinarjam.com/6mn333/go/live/0qgxxxa87i1s7s2. Worth knowing because any capture that scrapes the visible page and stops there gets nothing.

21 objections, answered on camera, before the call

Each video is titled with the objection it kills: do I need followers, what if I can't get approved, what's the risk, will this work if I have a job. She is answering the sales conversation before it happens, at 4K, on the page every registrant lands on.

Risk is reframed as inaction

“The real risk is not the program. The real risk is hesitating while one of the biggest opportunities in social media history is happening in front of us.” She never defends the price — she moves the risk onto standing still.

She refuses to promise income

The 90-day video says outright “how much money will you make in 90 days? It's impossible to tell” and substitutes three process wins: confidence, first commissions, and a durable skill. Legally clean and more believable than a number.

The honesty pledge is a verbal tic with a job

Nearly every answer opens with “I want to be so honest with you” or “I always want to be honest with you.” It buys permission to then deliver a partially inconvenient answer, which makes the convenient part land harder.

Concede the hard fact, then reveal the workaround

On followers she concedes TikTok's real 5,000-follower rule, then reveals a “cheat code” for pre-approved accounts. The concession is what makes the reveal credible.

Two-step upsell ladder

Opt-in, then $27 VIP, then a separate downsell for replay access, then confirmation. She monetises the decline, not just the acceptance.
